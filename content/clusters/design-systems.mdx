A design system is easy to describe in the abstract, a shared library of components, tokens, and patterns, and hard to actually own well. The gap between the two is most of what this cluster is about.

**These posts are grouped by where you actually are in a design system's life, not by an idealized org chart of what a "mature" system should look like.** Most teams don't get to design a system from a blank slate with unlimited buy-in. They inherit one, half-build one under deadline pressure, or realize partway through a product's growth that inconsistency has quietly become a real cost.

[What I Learned Building a Design System From Scratch](/blog/design-systems-at-scale) covers the earliest and highest-leverage phase: the decisions that are cheap to make early and expensive to unwind later, made under the actual constraints of a real project rather than a green-field exercise.

[Icons Are the Most Underestimated Part of Your Design System](/blog/icons-design-systems) is deliberately narrow. Icon systems get treated as decoration until they're not: inconsistent icon logic is one of the fastest ways a "consistent" design system stops feeling consistent to the people using it every day, and it's an easy piece to get right early.

[Design Systems: What Real Design System Experience Actually Looks Like](/blog/design-systems-applied) is the honest version of the other two: what ownership looks like once a system is live, being used by other people, and has to survive contact with a real product roadmap instead of a design system pitch deck.

**Two topics that belong in this cluster and don't have their own article yet: governance (who can propose, approve, and deprecate a component, and how that decision-making actually scales past one designer) and accessibility (how a design system either bakes accessibility in as a default or leaves every team to rediscover the same WCAG issues independently).** Both are real, both come up constantly in practice, and both deserve their own write-up rather than a rushed paragraph here. They'll be added to this cluster once written.

Case studies applying this thinking to a live product will be added here as they're ready to publish.
