## 60-Second Summary

**The problem:** Every launch post, App Store page, and portfolio piece needs a device mockup. Most tools that make them ask for **an account, a subscription, or an upload of your unreleased screenshots** before you get a single image out.

**What I built:** [MockSnap](https://mocksnap.app/), a free device mockup generator that runs entirely in the browser.

- **Drop in screenshots, pick a device, layout, and background, export.** That's the whole flow.
- **No sign-up.** No account stands between the user and the download.
- **Nothing is uploaded.** Screenshots stay on the user's machine.

**My role:** Solo. I framed the problem, scoped the product, designed the UX and UI, built it, and shipped it.

**Outcome:** <mark>Live and free at mocksnap.app.</mark> A Pro tier is planned, with a waitlist already open.

## The Problem

Mockups are one of those small jobs that come up **every single time** something ships: a launch tweet, a Product Hunt page, a Dribbble shot, a case study cover. The job itself is simple. The tools around it often aren't.

  caption="A finished MockSnap export, made in seconds from two StreamShare screenshots. This is the image a launch post needs."

Common friction points:

- **Accounts and paywalls** in front of basic exports
- **Uploading screenshots to a server**, which is a real concern when the screens belong to a product that hasn't launched yet
- **Guessing output sizes** for each platform, then resizing afterwards

> **How do you make a five-minute job actually take five minutes, without asking people to trade away their screenshots or their email address?**

I ran into this on my own portfolio. Case studies like [StreamShare](/work/streamshare-streaming-app-design) needed clean device mockups, and the job kept taking longer than it should. So I built the tool I wanted to use.

## Who It's For

The site states it plainly: **indie developers, designers, and anyone launching something.**

These users share a few traits:

- They need a **good-looking image fast**, usually right before posting something
- They often work with **unreleased screens**, so privacy matters
- They publish to **many different places**, each with its own image size

## Key Design Decisions

### 1. Browser-Only Processing

Screenshots are processed **in the browser and never uploaded**.

- Unreleased app screens never leave the user's machine
- No upload step means no waiting on a server
- <mark>Privacy is a property of the architecture, not a promise in a policy page</mark>

### 2. No Sign-Up, Free Core

Every device, layout, background, and export option is **free, and the site commits to keeping it free**.

- The first thing a user does is the actual job, not a registration form
- Monetization is planned as an **optional Pro tier** on top, not a paywall around the basics

> _A tool you reach for once a month has to earn trust in the first ten seconds. A sign-up wall spends that trust before it's earned._

### 3. A Small Set of Strong Choices

  src="/images/work/mocksnap/mocksnap-streamshare-curated-controls-hero.webp"

Instead of an open-ended canvas, MockSnap offers **curated options** that cover most launch needs:

| Choice          | Options                                         |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Devices**     | iPhone, Android, tablet, browser window, laptop |
| **Layouts**     | Flat, tilted, isometric, cascade, fan           |
| **Backgrounds** | Twelve gradients, solid, image, or transparent  |
| **Text**        | Built-in title and subtitle                     |

Fewer, better defaults mean **less time deciding and more time shipping**.

### 4. Export Presets Mapped to Destinations

<MediaFigure
  src="/images/work/mocksnap/mocksnap-streamshare-export-destinations-hero.webp"
  alt="The same two-phone StreamShare mockup exported in three formats side by side: Slides 16:9, Post 1:1, and Stories 9:16 with the phones stacked vertically"

Users don't think in pixels. They think **"this is for Instagram"** or **"this goes in my slides."**

- Presets for **16:9 slides, Dribbble, Instagram, Stories, link previews, and X headers**, plus custom sizes
- **PNG, JPG, or lossless WebP** at 1x, 2x, or 3x, up to 5760 px
- **Copy to clipboard** for pasting straight into a post or doc

## How It's Built

I designed and built MockSnap myself, which meant design decisions could go all the way down to the rendering code.

- **One render function for preview and export.** The preview and the downloaded file are drawn by the same code, so <mark>what you see is exactly what you get</mark>
- **Real 3D perspective.** Tilted layouts are warped onto the canvas with WebGL2, which keeps perspective correct and screenshots sharp
- **Autosave without an account.** Settings live in local storage and images in IndexedDB, so work survives a refresh and never leaves the device
- **Built for keyboards too.** Shortcuts for undo, redo, download, paste, and the sidebar, and a layout that works down to 320px
- **Two small deployments.** The landing page and the editor each run as their own Cloudflare Worker, and the Pro waitlist is stored in Cloudflare D1

## Reflection

On client work, the brief, the budget, and the roadmap belong to someone else. On MockSnap, **every trade-off was mine**, and that changed how I made them.

**Scope was the hardest design problem.**

- Every extra option looked useful on its own: more devices, more layouts, a free-form canvas
- Each one also added a decision for someone who just wants an image before they post
- Keeping the choices **small and curated** meant saying no to features I would have happily designed for a client

**The most important decisions don't appear on any screen.**

- *Where screenshots are processed* and *whether an account is needed* shaped the experience more than any layout or color choice
- <mark>Owning the build meant I could make privacy a property of the architecture</mark>, instead of handing a spec to engineering and hoping it survived

**Using it on my own work kept me honest.**

- Every image on this page was made in MockSnap from my [StreamShare](/work/streamshare-streaming-app-design) concept screens
- Using the tool on a real portfolio piece showed me quickly what felt slow and what was missing

**What's next:** MockSnap Pro, built on top of a core that stays free.

> *Designing for a client tests whether I can solve someone else's problem. Shipping my own product tests whether I can decide what's worth solving.*

## Key Takeaways

- **Privacy can be a design decision.** Choosing where processing happens shaped the product more than any screen did.
- **Removing steps is a feature.** No sign-up and no upload are the core of the experience, not missing pieces.
- **Shipping closes the loop.** Designing something real and putting it in front of people tests decisions in a way a concept never can.
