## Overview

During lockdown, I fell into the social media scroll trap like everyone else. One night, I stumbled onto a local band jamming live on a streaming platform. Hours disappeared. It wasn't just entertainment. It was real human connection: watching, chatting, discovering something I didn't know I was looking for.

But existing streaming apps made that kind of find nearly impossible to repeat. <mark>Navigation was confusing. Categories were nonexistent. Discovery felt like luck, not design.</mark>

This case study explores what a thoughtfully designed streaming app could look like if discovery were treated as a first-class feature.

## My Role & Scope

Solo project, end to end: research, competitive analysis, user interviews, personas, user flows, wireframes, usability testing, visual design, and prototyping.

Built in Balsamiq, Miro, and Adobe XD. The goal wasn't to ship to the App Store. It was to explore what intentional streaming discovery could look like when someone actually designed it.

## Research & Discovery

I interviewed 5 potential users aged 20–40. Across the conversations, three patterns kept surfacing:

- People don't just want entertainment. They want content that **matches their actual interests**, not a trending feed of things they've never clicked on.
- **Location-based streams** (local bands, workshops, neighbourhood events) felt novel and personal in a way global recommendations didn't.
- Existing apps felt cluttered. Users wanted clarity, not more content.

> "I don't want more stuff to watch. I want less stuff that's actually for me."

### Competitive Analysis

Competitors (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube) all had live features. None combined:

- Interest-based recommendations
- Location awareness
- Stream scheduling

<mark>That gap became the design opportunity.</mark> Not a missing feature. A missing combination.

### User Personas

**Rakesh** (student): Wants to discover content aligned with his hobbies without wading through irrelevant feeds.

**Vijay** (travel and learning): Interested in location-based streams. Local music, cultural events, city workshops.

**Prajina** (creator): Needs scheduling tools and discoverability beyond her existing follower base.

## Design Solution

Rather than chasing every feature, I focused on three core capabilities.

### 1. Interest-Based Personalization

Users choose categories during signup. The home feed recommends live and upcoming streams in those interests. <mark>No algorithm mystery. Clear, relevant content from the first open.</mark>

This removed the cold-start problem: new users see useful content immediately rather than a generic trending feed.

### 2. Location Discovery

A map-based browse mode lets users find streams happening nearby. A local band, a neighbourhood workshop, a city event. Creators tag streams with a location at scheduling time.

> Location-based content felt personal and novel in a way that global trending feeds didn't. Research confirmed it. The map tab reflected it.

### 3. Stream Scheduling

Creators can schedule upcoming streams with titles, descriptions, categories, and location tags. Viewers can follow a creator and get notified before the stream starts.

<mark>This solved a key friction: discovering a creator you liked often meant missing their next live because there was no way to know when they'd go live.</mark> Scheduling changed the dynamic from reactive to intentional.

## Wireframes & Flows

Core flows designed:

1. **Onboarding**: Category selection (3–5 interests), optional location permission
2. **Home feed**: Interest-matched live and upcoming streams, one-tap join
3. **Map view**: Location-based stream discovery with filter by category
4. **Stream detail**: Creator info, chat, upcoming schedule, related streams
5. **Creator scheduling**: Title, description, category, time, location

Navigation: 4 tabs (Home, Discover, Schedule, Profile). No buried settings. No feature sprawl.

## Key Design Decisions

**Interest selection at onboarding, not later.** Surfacing preferences upfront meant the app was useful from the first open. Users who set interests during onboarding were noticeably more engaged in usability tests than those who skipped it.

**Map as a first-class feature.** Most streaming apps treat location as metadata. Here it's a primary browse mode. The map tab sits next to Home in the nav bar, signalling it's a core path.

**Scheduled streams over surprise-only live.** <mark>Scheduling let creators build an audience before going live. It shifted the app from reactive (scroll until something good appears) to intentional (bookmark a stream for Thursday at 8pm).</mark>

## Usability Testing

Three rounds of informal testing with 4–5 participants each. Key findings:

- **Onboarding friction.** First iteration asked users to select from 20+ categories. Reduced to curated top 10 with a search option. Completion rate improved significantly.
- **Map confusion.** Early map view showed all streams globally by default. Added a "near me" toggle as the default state. Users grasped the feature much faster.
- **Schedule discoverability.** Users didn't notice the schedule tab at first. Added scheduling entry points on creator profiles, not just the tab bar.

## Outcome

This project didn't ship. But the process sharpened how I think about discovery-first design, interest-based personalization, and the underexplored space of location-aware content.

> Most streaming platforms are built around creators going live and hoping an audience finds them. A small shift (giving viewers tools to find, follow, and schedule) changes the dynamic entirely.

The insight that stuck most: <mark>the problem wasn't content. It was the infrastructure around finding it.</mark> That's a design problem. And it's solvable.
