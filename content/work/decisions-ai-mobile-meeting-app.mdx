## A note on this page

I want to be upfront about what this page is and isn't. It isn't a research-to-outcome case study, the kind where I walk you through a problem, what I learned, and the decisions that followed. I can't write that page honestly without exposing client conversations, internal data, and product reasoning that's covered under an active NDA.

What follows instead is a straightforward account of the product I work on, and the role I actually hold, built entirely from public sources plus my own description of my job.

## About Decisions AI

Decisions is an AI-powered meeting management platform built natively for **Microsoft Teams** and **Microsoft 365**. It covers the full meeting lifecycle:

- **Before** — agenda building
- **During** — decision and task logging
- **After** — AI-generated recaps and minutes

It integrates directly with Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneNote, SharePoint, and Microsoft's Copilot and OpenAI infrastructure, and it's built for organizations that need everything to stay inside their own Microsoft 365 tenant.

My focus within the broader product is **Decisions AI**, the mobile companion app.

Per the public listings and support documentation, the app includes:

- Agenda building and templates
- Real-time decision and task logging
- Secure in-meeting voting
- AI Recap, AI Minutes, and an AI Agenda Assistant
- Task sync and assignment
- A document viewer with text selection and annotation
- Meeting transcription support for in-person meetings

The company's own site lists **5,000+ organizations** on the platform, including Vestas, BDO, NHS, Beiersdorf, and Manpower, with use cases spanning leadership teams, boards, audit committees, and government bodies.

Decisions does have a free tier, the Small Teams plan, for up to 5 users — but it's only available to organizations already on Microsoft 365 with 30 or fewer users on their tenant, for the same reason: no personal freemium path exists.

## My role

**Lead Product Designer, sole designer on the mobile app since it started.**

I own the entire UX/UI design process for the mobile app — everything short of writing the code:

- **Feature research** — competitive analysis, sitting in on client calls directly, and gathering feedback that feeds into design direction
- **End-to-end design** — wireframing when a flow genuinely calls for it, interaction design, visual design, and prototyping
- **Iteration** — design, ship, observe how it lands, redesign
- **Product management** — for most of this project, I've also written product specs, managed the backlog, and been the primary point of contact for client feedback, feature requests, and concerns, alongside my own data analysis to back up direction. A dedicated PM joined recently as the workload grew.

The team otherwise is lean: two developers dedicated to mobile, with additional engineers rotating in and out over time.

One recent shift worth naming: I've started using AI tools, **Claude and ChatGPT**, as part of my own process — for early design direction, quick prototyping, interaction ideas, copywriting, and competitive analysis. It's changed how fast I can move on the parts of the job that used to take longest just to get started on.
