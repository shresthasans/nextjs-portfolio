## A note on this page

I want to be direct about what this page is and isn't. It isn't the kind of case study where I walk you through a problem, what I learned, and the decisions that followed. I can't write that page honestly without exposing client conversations, internal data, and product reasoning that's covered under an active NDA.

What follows instead is a straightforward account of the product I work on, and the role I actually hold, built entirely from public sources plus my own description of my job. The signal I'd point you to instead: an active enterprise client has kept me as its only mobile designer for seven years and grown my scope into product management along the way. That's not a research narrative, but it's not nothing either.

## About Decisions AI

Decisions is an [AI-powered meeting management platform](/ai-ux-design-nepal) built natively for **Microsoft Teams** and **Microsoft 365**, and a two-time Microsoft App Award winner (1st place, Best Office Integration, 2018; 3rd place, Best Overall Apps, 2019). It covers the full meeting lifecycle:

- **Before:** agenda building
- **During:** decision and task logging
- **After:** AI-generated recaps and minutes

It integrates directly with Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneNote, SharePoint, and Microsoft's Copilot and OpenAI infrastructure, and it's built for organizations that need everything to stay inside their own Microsoft 365 tenant.

My focus within the broader product is **Decisions AI**, the mobile companion app: the one surface I've designed from the ground up since I joined in 2019. I also own UX/UI for the platform's other integrated surfaces (Teams, Outlook, and the rest of Microsoft 365), which already had interfaces in place when I joined: reusing components where they hold up, designing new ones where something genuinely new is needed, then reviewing the implementation and iterating on it after it ships.

Per the public listings and support documentation, the app includes:

- Agenda building and templates
- Real-time decision and task logging
- Secure in-meeting voting
- AI Recap, AI Minutes, and an AI Agenda Assistant
- Task sync and assignment
- A document viewer with text selection and annotation
- Meeting transcription support for in-person meetings

The company's own site lists **5,000+ organizations** on the platform, with use cases spanning leadership teams, boards, audit committees, and government bodies.

Decisions does have a free tier, the Small Teams plan, for up to 5 users, but it's only available to organizations already on Microsoft 365 with 30 or fewer users on their tenant, for the same reason: no personal freemium path exists.

## My role

**Lead Product Designer: sole designer on the mobile app since it started, and owner of UX/UI across the platform's other integrated surfaces as well.**

I own the UX/UI design process across the product, building mobile from the ground up and extending, adding to, and reviewing design on the platform's other integrated surfaces. That's everything short of writing the code:

- **Feature research:** competitive analysis, sitting in on client calls directly, and gathering feedback that feeds into design direction
- **End-to-end design:** wireframing when a flow genuinely calls for it, interaction design, visual design, and prototyping
- **Iteration:** design, ship, observe how it lands, redesign
- **Product management:** for most of this project, I've also written product specs, managed the backlog, and been the primary point of contact for client feedback, feature requests, and concerns, alongside my own data analysis to back up direction

That last point is the one I'd ask a reader to sit with: a team that starts as "one designer, two developers" doesn't stay that shape for seven years, and doesn't hand its designer the backlog and the client relationship, unless the work is holding up. A dedicated PM joined recently as the workload grew. It's the first time in the project's history the design and PM functions haven't sat with one person.

The team otherwise stays lean: two developers dedicated to mobile, with additional engineers rotating in and out over time.

One recent shift worth naming: I've started using AI tools, **Claude, ChatGPT, V0, Lovable, and Figma's AI features (Figma Make, Chat Agents)**, as part of my own process, for early design direction, quick prototyping, interaction ideas, copywriting, and competitive analysis. It's changed how fast I can move on the parts of the job that used to take longest just to get started on.
