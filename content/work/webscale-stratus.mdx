## Overview

Webscale Stratus is a cloud infrastructure platform purpose-built for eCommerce, think smart CDN, traffic management, and performance optimisation as a managed service. When I joined as Senior Product Designer, the platform was technically powerful but visually and experientially dated, with an onboarding drop-off rate that was silently costing the company significant ARR.

## My Role

Over 2.5 years I owned:

- End-to-end UX for the core platform (onboarding, dashboard, alerts, configuration)
- Building Stratus Design System from scratch, the first design system the company had
- User research programme: quarterly usability testing, session recording analysis, support ticket triage
- Design operations: aligning with 4 frontend engineers across US and India timezones

## The Challenge

Webscale's customers are enterprise eCommerce teams, technically sophisticated, time-poor, and under constant pressure from trading peaks (Black Friday, Cyber Monday). The platform had to serve both a DevOps engineer tuning performance configurations and a VP of eCommerce checking uptime before a flash sale.

Key problems:

- **Onboarding**: New customers required extensive hand-holding from solutions engineers because the self-serve setup was opaque. The journey from sign-up to first live site was 14 days average, with most of that time waiting for humans.
- **Dashboard complexity**: The main dashboard surfaced 80+ metrics with no hierarchy. Users couldn't tell what was important.
- **Alert fatigue**: The alerting system generated noise. Customers had trained themselves to ignore alerts, a genuine risk during actual incidents.

## Process

### Research First

I audited 6 months of support tickets and categorised every issue. 42% of tickets related to onboarding configuration, things that should have been self-explanatory. I ran moderated usability tests with 8 customers and identified 3 critical failure points in the first-run experience.

### Onboarding Redesign

The old onboarding was a wizard with 12 steps, most of which required information the customer didn't have at hand (DNS records, origin IPs, SSL certificates). We restructured it as an asynchronous journey:

- Collect only what's needed right now
- "Park" steps that need backend configuration, with email reminders
- Progress saved automatically; resume from any device
- Contextual help embedded in-line, not in a separate help centre

Time-to-first-live-site dropped from 14 days to 3 days. Onboarding conversion improved by 60%.

### Dashboard Hierarchy

I introduced a three-layer architecture: **Health** (is anything broken?), **Performance** (how fast is the site?), **Traffic** (what's the pattern?). Each layer had a progressive detail model, a single health score at a glance, with drill-down for root cause.

The 80+ metrics were organised into these three lenses. Only 8 metrics were surfaced at the top level; everything else was one click away with context about why it mattered.

### Design System

Stratus Design System shipped with:

- 200+ components across web and email
- Design tokens for colour, spacing, typography
- Figma + Storybook paired documentation
- Accessibility annotation kit for engineering handoff
- Contribution guidelines so product teams could extend it

The design system reduced designer-to-engineer friction significantly and cut UI-related QA cycles in half.
